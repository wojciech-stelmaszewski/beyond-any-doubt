# Beyond Any Doubt — Design & Technology Plan

- **Session timestamp:** 2026-09-04_11-49
- **Project:** `beyond-any-doubt` — an advanced mathematics blog
- **Production domain:** `beyond-any-doubt.org`
- **Hosting:** GitHub Pages (static, custom domain)
- **Status:** Proposal — awaiting user review. No code generated yet.

---

## 1. Goals and Constraints

| Requirement | Consequence for the design |
| --- | --- |
| Long-form, cognitively demanding reading | Typography and contrast tuned for sustained reading, not for marketing impact |
| Advanced LaTeX (aligned blocks, custom macros, cross-referenced equations) | Math renderer must be build-time and support numbering/referencing |
| High-quality charts | Charts must be part of the design system, not an afterthought; SVG-first, theme-aware |
| GitHub Pages | Fully static output, no server runtime, no server-side rendering at request time |
| Custom apex domain | `CNAME` file + DNS `ALIAS`/`A` records, HTTPS enforced |

Two constraints dominate every decision below:

1. **The content is the product.** Colour must encode structure (definition, theorem, proof, warning) and must never compete with the mathematics for attention.
2. **Everything ships static.** Any technique that needs a server at request time is out of scope.

## 2. Evidence Base

Colour advice on the web is mostly folklore. The palette below is derived from findings that are
either well replicated or are established accessibility standards. Each claim is labelled with the
strength of its evidence, so that weak effects are not mistaken for strong ones.

### 2.1 Strong evidence — drives the core decisions

**Reading speed saturates far below maximum contrast.**
Legge, G. E., Rubin, G. S., & Luebker, A. (1987). *Psychophysics of reading—V. The role of contrast
in normal vision.* Vision Research, 27(7), 1165–1177.
Reading rate rises steeply with contrast at low levels and then plateaus; beyond roughly an order of
magnitude above threshold there is no further gain.
**Consequence:** pure `#000000` on `#FFFFFF` buys no measurable reading benefit over a near-black ink
on a warm off-white, while maximising glare and halation. We use `#16181D` on `#FAF8F4`
(contrast 16.7:1) instead of 21:1.

**Positive polarity (dark text on light background) outperforms negative polarity.**
- Buchner, A., & Baumgartner, N. (2007). *Text–background polarity affects performance irrespective
  of ambient illumination and colour contrast.* Ergonomics, 50(7), 1036–1063.
- Piepenbrock, C., Mayr, S., Mund, I., & Buchner, A. (2013). *Positive display polarity is
  advantageous for both younger and older adults.* Ergonomics, 56(7), 1116–1124.
- Piepenbrock, C., Mayr, S., & Buchner, A. (2014). *Smaller pupil size and better proofreading
  performance with positive than with negative polarity displays.* Ergonomics, 57(11), 1670–1677.

The 2014 paper identifies the mechanism: light backgrounds constrict the pupil, which increases
depth of field and retinal acuity.
**Consequence:** light theme is the **default**. Dark theme is offered but not preferred.

**Some readers genuinely need the opposite.**
Legge, G. E., Rubin, G. S., Pelli, D. G., & Schleske, M. M. (1985). *Psychophysics of reading—II.
Low vision.* Vision Research, 25(2), 253–265.
A substantial subset of low-vision readers read faster with reversed polarity.
**Consequence:** dark theme is a first-class, fully specified theme — not a filter applied to the
light theme — and the toggle respects `prefers-color-scheme` with a persisted manual override.

**Colour-vision deficiency affects roughly 8% of men of Northern European descent.**
- Okabe, M., & Ito, K. (2008). *Color Universal Design (CUD): How to make figures and presentations
  that are friendly to colorblind people.*
- Wong, B. (2011). *Points of view: Color blindness.* Nature Methods, 8(6), 441.

**Consequence:** the chart palette is built on Okabe–Ito, and colour is never the sole carrier of
meaning (also a WCAG 2.2 requirement, SC 1.4.1).

**Rainbow/`jet` colour maps distort quantitative data.**
Crameri, F., Shephard, G. E., & Heron, P. J. (2020). *The misuse of colour in science
communication.* Nature Communications, 11, 5444.
Non-uniform luminance ramps create perceptual gradients that are not in the data.
**Consequence:** continuous data uses perceptually uniform maps only (`viridis`, `cividis`,
`batlow`); diverging data uses a balanced diverging map anchored at a meaningful zero.

**Working memory for simultaneously tracked items is about four.**
Cowan, N. (2001). *The magical number 4 in short-term memory: A reconsideration of mental storage
capacity.* Behavioral and Brain Sciences, 24(1), 87–114.
**Consequence:** at most four colour-coded semantic categories are visible on a page at once, and
the default chart series limit is five (four hues plus ink), after which redundant encoding becomes
mandatory.

**Extraneous visual detail increases cognitive load and harms learning.**
- Sweller, J., van Merriënboer, J. J. G., & Paas, F. (1998). *Cognitive architecture and
  instructional design.* Educational Psychology Review, 10(3), 251–296.
- Mayer, R. E. (2009). *Multimedia Learning* (2nd ed.) — coherence and signalling principles.

**Consequence:** no decorative colour. Every hue on the page has a defined semantic job.

### 2.2 Weaker / contested evidence — informs taste, not structure

**Ambient red may impair performance on achievement tasks.**
Elliot, A. J., Maier, M. A., Moller, A. C., Friedman, R., & Meinhardt, J. (2007). *Color and
psychological functioning: The effect of red on performance attainment.* Journal of Experimental
Psychology: General, 136(1), 154–168.

**Blue may favour exploratory thinking, red detail-oriented thinking.**
Mehta, R., & Zhu, R. (2009). *Blue or Red? Exploring the Effect of Color on Cognitive Task
Performances.* Science, 323(5918), 1226–1229.

Both belong to the colour-psychology literature, which has a poor replication record; effect sizes
are small and context-dependent. They are **not** load-bearing here. They only tip an
already-reasonable choice: the primary accent is a deep indigo rather than a red, and red is
reserved for genuinely negative semantics (counterexample, error). That choice is independently
justified by convention and by the need to keep error signalling unambiguous.

### 2.3 Standards applied

- **WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.3 (AA):** ≥ 4.5:1 for body text — all text tokens meet this.
- **WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.6 (AAA):** ≥ 7:1 — primary text, links and most semantic colours meet this.
- **WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.11:** ≥ 3:1 for non-text graphical objects — all default chart series meet this.
- **WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.1:** colour is never the only visual means of conveying information.

## 3. The Palette — "Ink & Proof"

The identity is a mathematical manuscript: warm paper, near-black ink, and a single disciplined
indigo used for anything the reader can act on. Semantic hues mark the structure of an argument.

### 3.6 Verified limits of this palette

The candidate palettes were run through a Viénot (1999) LMS colour-vision-deficiency simulation and
scored by pairwise CIE-Lab distance. The results set honest boundaries on what the palette can
promise:

| Series count | Deuteranopia min ΔE | Protanopia min ΔE | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5 (light, default) | 16.6 (green/purple) | 22.3 (blue/purple) | Usable — distinguishable, not comfortable |
| 5 (dark, default) | 17.1 (green/purple) | 15.6 (blue/purple) | Usable |
| 8 (full Okabe–Ito) | 7.5 (vermillion/gold) | 2.9 (vermillion/gold) | **Fails** — indistinguishable pairs |

Two conclusions follow, and they are firm rules rather than suggestions:

1. **Cap the default at five series.** This coincides with Cowan's (2001) working-memory limit, so
   the accessibility constraint and the cognitive constraint agree.
2. **Above five series, colour alone is insufficient** — dash pattern, marker symbol and direct
   labelling become mandatory. This is exactly what WCAG SC 1.4.1 requires anyway.

An attempt to force every series above 4.5:1 was tested and **rejected**: it drives all series
toward the same relative luminance (0.14–0.19), which destroys greyscale separation and makes the
charts unreadable when printed in black and white. Varied luminance plus redundant encoding is the
better trade.

Note also that the tritanopia figures from this simulation are unreliable — the Viénot method is
calibrated for protanopia and deuteranopia. Tritanopia is rare (well under 0.1%), and Okabe–Ito was
designed with it in mind, but the palette should still be checked in a Brettel-based simulator
before launch.

### 3.7 Continuous and diverging data

- **Sequential:** `viridis` (perceptually uniform) or `cividis` (additionally optimised for
  deuteranopia).
- **Diverging:** a balanced blue–orange or blue–red map anchored at a meaningful zero. Never a
  rainbow map — see Crameri et al. (2020).
- **Always** show the colour bar, and prefer explicit contour lines over colour alone for
  quantitative reading.

### 3.8 Implementation notes

- Tokens are defined once as CSS custom properties on `:root` and overridden under
  `[data-theme="dark"]`. Components never reference a raw hex value.
- Ramps are authored in **OKLCH**, which is perceptually uniform in lightness, so that tints and
  shades stay visually even. Hex values above are the sRGB fallbacks and remain the source of truth
  for contrast calculations.
- The focus ring uses `--accent` at 3px with a 2px offset — visible against both `--paper` and
  `--paper-sunken`.

## 4. Typography

Colour and type are one system; the palette is tuned assuming these settings.

| Role | Choice | Rationale |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Body | Source Serif 4 (variable) | Serif supports long-form reading and sits comfortably beside maths |
| Headings / UI | Inter (variable) | Neutral, excellent at small sizes, wide numeral support |
| Code | JetBrains Mono | Clear disambiguation of `0/O`, `1/l/I` |
| Maths | KaTeX default (Computer Modern) | Familiar to a mathematical audience |

- Body size ~19–20px with line height 1.65; measure capped at ~68 characters
  (Legge & Bigelow, 2011, on print size; Dyson, 2004, on line length).
- Fonts are self-hosted and subset, preloaded as WOFF2. No third-party font CDN — it costs a
  connection and leaks reader IPs.
- **Open question:** if perfect text/maths harmony matters more than familiarity, STIX Two Text +
  STIX Two Math (with MathJax) is the alternative. This is a taste decision for the review.

## 5. Technology

### 5.1 Recommended stack

| Layer | Choice |
| --- | --- |
| Framework | **Astro 7** (static output) |
| Content | Markdown + MDX, in typed content collections |
| Markdown engine | **Sätteri** (Astro 7 default, Rust) with `features.math` |
| Maths | **KaTeX**, rendered at build time via `satteri-katex` on MDAST |
| Charts | **Observable Plot**, server-side rendered to SVG at build time |
| Code | Shiki (built into Astro), dual light/dark themes |
| Search | Pagefind (static index, no backend) |
| Comments | Giscus (GitHub Discussions), optional and opt-in |
| CI/CD | GitHub Actions → `actions/deploy-pages` |

### 5.2 Why Astro

Astro emits pure static HTML with zero client-side JavaScript by default, and adds JavaScript only
for components explicitly marked as interactive ("islands"). For a blog that is 95% prose and
equations with occasional interactive figures, this is exactly the right shape. Astro 7 (released
22 June 2026) additionally requires Node ≥ 22.12 — the local environment is on 22.21.1, so no
upgrade is needed.

### 5.3 The Astro 7 markdown caveat — read before starting

Astro 7 replaced the unified (remark/rehype) markdown pipeline with **Sätteri**, a Rust processor.
**remark and rehype plugins do not run by default.** For a maths blog this is the single most
important detail in the whole stack, because the conventional recipe (`remark-math` +
`rehype-katex`) is exactly what stops working. There are two viable routes:

| Route | How | Trade-off |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A. Native Sätteri** (recommended) | `features: { math: true }` + `satteri-katex` in `mdastPlugins` | Fast builds; depends on a young community plugin ecosystem |
| **B. Unified compatibility** | Install `@astrojs/markdown-remark`, keep `remarkPlugins`/`rehypePlugins` | Battle-tested plugin ecosystem; officially deprecated, emits warnings |

There is a documented trap in route A: `satteri-katex` must be registered in **`mdastPlugins`, not
`hastPlugins`**. The syntax highlighter runs first, and on HAST a display-maths block is still a
code node — so it gets highlighted as a plaintext code block and silently never reaches a HAST-level
maths plugin. The failure is visual, not an error.

**Recommendation:** start with route A and keep route B as a documented, one-commit fallback. A
spike task exists specifically to validate this before committing to the stack (see task 2.1).

### 5.4 Maths renderer: KaTeX vs MathJax 4

| Criterion | KaTeX | MathJax 4 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Build-time rendering | Yes | Yes |
| Client JS needed | **None** | None if pre-rendered |
| Bundle / CSS | ~200 KB + fonts, external CSS | Self-contained, larger |
| LaTeX coverage | Large subset | Substantially more complete |
| `\label` / `\eqref` | **Not supported** | Native (`tagformat`, `tags: 'ams'`) |
| Accessibility | Static MathML + ARIA | Interactive explorer, speech, braille |

For a blog described as "fairly advanced", equation cross-referencing is a real requirement, and
this is the one place KaTeX genuinely falls short.

**Recommendation:** begin with KaTeX plus an explicit numbering convention (`\tag{...}` and manual
anchors), because it is faster, lighter and ships zero JavaScript. Treat MathJax 4 as a
pre-committed migration path, to be taken the first time a post is blocked by a missing package or
by cross-referencing friction. To keep that door open, all maths goes through a single component
boundary so the renderer can be swapped in one place. A `satteri-mathjax` plugin exists in the same
plugin family, so the swap is bounded.

### 5.5 Charts

Observable Plot is a concise grammar-of-graphics layer over D3. Critically, it accepts a `document`
option, so with `linkedom` (lighter than JSDOM) it renders to an SVG string **at build time**. The
default path therefore ships a plain, theme-aware, crawlable, printable SVG and **no JavaScript at
all**.

Interactive figures (sliders, zoom, live parameters) become Astro islands, hydrated with
`client:visible` so they cost nothing until scrolled into view. D3 remains available directly for
bespoke visualisations that Plot's grammar cannot express.

Charts read their colours from the CSS custom properties above, so light/dark switching needs no
re-render and no duplicated chart definitions.

### 5.6 Alternative considered: Quarto

Quarto is a strong fit for technical writing — native theorem/proof environments, BibTeX citations,
automatic cross-references, and executable Python/R/Julia code producing figures. It was rejected as
the primary stack because design control is comparatively limited and the toolchain is heavier,
while this project explicitly wants a distinctive visual identity and interactive figures. It
remains the right answer if the workflow turns out to be notebook-driven; this is worth a explicit
decision during review.

### 5.7 Deployment to GitHub Pages with an apex domain

- `public/CNAME` containing `beyond-any-doubt.org`.
- DNS: apex `A`/`AAAA` records to GitHub Pages IPs (or `ALIAS`/`ANAME` if the registrar supports
  it), plus a `www` `CNAME` to the GitHub Pages host.
- Repository Settings → Pages → Source: **GitHub Actions**.
- Enforce HTTPS once the certificate is issued.
- Because the site is served from an apex domain, `base` stays `/` — no repository-subpath handling
  is needed. `site` is set to `https://beyond-any-doubt.org` so canonical URLs, the sitemap and RSS
  are correct.
- Known limitation: GitHub Pages controls its own `Cache-Control` headers and currently serves
  hashed assets with a short lifetime that the site cannot override. Acceptable; worth revisiting
  only if it shows up in real metrics.

## 6. Risks

| Risk | Impact | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `satteri-katex` is a young community plugin | Build breaks or maths regressions | Spike first (task 2.1); documented fallback to `@astrojs/markdown-remark` |
| KaTeX cannot express needed notation | Blocked posts | Single maths component boundary; MathJax 4 migration path pre-scoped |
| Colour palette underperforms in real use | Rework after launch | Review against a rendered preview and a CVD simulator before implementation |
| Astro 7 is recent | Ecosystem gaps | Node 22.21.1 already satisfies it; pin exact versions and upgrade deliberately |
| Scope creep (search, comments, analytics) | Launch delay | Phase 4 is explicitly optional and post-launch |

## 7. Alignment with Core Principles

- **KISS:** one static framework, one set of CSS custom properties, no state management library, no
  CSS framework, no runtime CMS.
- **YAGNI:** search, comments and analytics are deferred to an optional post-launch phase. No
  multi-language support, no tag taxonomy, no author system until a second author exists.
- **SRP:** design tokens live in one file; the maths renderer sits behind one component boundary;
  chart rendering is one build-time utility.
- **DRY:** applied only to the token layer, where duplication would guarantee drift. Post content
  and component markup stay explicit and readable.

## 8. Decisions Required From the User

1. **Palette:** approve "Ink & Proof" as specified, or request adjustments to hue/warmth.
2. **Maths renderer:** KaTeX first (recommended), or go straight to MathJax 4 for `\eqref`.
3. **Markdown route:** native Sätteri (recommended), or the unified compatibility layer.
4. **Typography:** Source Serif 4 + KaTeX (recommended), or STIX Two Text + STIX Two Math.
5. **Framework:** Astro (recommended), or Quarto if the workflow is notebook-driven.

Implementation starts only after these are settled.
